# Reporting and analysis

These are questions about the shape of the books rather than a single record: what is owed, what changed, what is overdue.

## Get the numbers from the ledger

Use whatever reporting the DualEntry connection exposes. If no report covers the question, build the answer from record searches instead, and say that is what you did, so the user knows whether they are reading an official report or a figure assembled from records.

Never estimate, extrapolate, or fill a gap from general knowledge. An accounting answer that is approximately right is wrong.

## Framing the answer

State the period and the company before the number. "Q3 2026, Acme Holdings: $184,200 outstanding across 23 invoices" is usable; "$184,200" alone is not.

Then give the breakdown that makes it actionable: the largest items, the aging buckets, or the movement since the comparison period. Lead with the figure the user asked for and keep the supporting detail below it.

## Comparisons

When comparing periods, say plainly what moved and by how much, in both absolute and percentage terms. Flag anything that makes the comparison unfair: a period that is not yet complete, a company added partway through, a currency that changed.

## Aging and what is overdue

Group by how far past due, count and total each bucket, and name the largest items. Distinguish overdue from merely outstanding. An invoice inside its terms is not a problem, and presenting it as one erodes trust in the whole answer.

## Be explicit about limits

If a question cannot be answered from the ledger, such as a forecast, a tax position, or a treatment that depends on policy, say so and describe what the books do show. Point the user to their accountant for judgment calls rather than offering one.
